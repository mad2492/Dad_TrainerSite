# Business Process

Purpose: capture how the training business works today before designing software around it.

## Current Workflow

### New Client Intake

- New clients usually find him through word of mouth from current clients.
- What does the first conversation look like?
- What questions does he always ask?
- Does he offer a consultation before paid sessions?
- How does he decide whether someone is a good fit?

### Training Sessions

- Where do sessions happen?
- How are appointments scheduled?
- How does he remember each client's workout history?
- What does he write down during or after sessions?
- What information does he need right before a session starts?

### Diets and Meal Plans

- How does he create diet plans today?
- Does he use templates or write each plan from scratch?
- How often does he adjust a client's plan?
- He most often sends clients diets and reminders.
- Rewriting or resending diets is currently annoying.
- What do clients most often misunderstand or forget?

### Check-Ins

- How often does he check in with clients?
- Does he ask for weight, measurements, photos, energy level, or compliance?
- Are check-ins scheduled or informal?
- What follow-up reminders would actually help?

### Payments

- How do clients pay today?
- Does he track who has paid?
- What payment method does he prefer?
- What payment problems, if any, happen repeatedly?

## Friction Points

Use this section for anything that feels messy, repetitive, or easy to lose.

- Diets are the main thing that gets annoying to resend or rewrite.
- Current growth is limited by in-person hours. One client can consume a large block of the day, while online clients could create more income without requiring the same schedule load.
- Dad sees online training as the part he does not yet know how to build.

## Things That Must Stay the Same

Some parts of the business may be personal, effective, or intentionally old-school. Capture those here.

- The one-on-one, face-to-face training should remain the same.
- The business should not become cookie-cutter. Programs should be based on each individual's needs.

## Online Training Opportunity

Dad's key business insight: in-person training is limited by the number of hours in a day, but online training could support 20 or 30 clients from home if it is simple enough to manage.

This means the platform should not only organize current clients. It should also help him create a manageable online coaching offer that preserves his customized approach.
