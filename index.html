A practical proposal

Fonseca Fitness Online Training

The idea has become clearer: Pete and Lisa Fonseca can build an online coaching program that feels personal, simple, and as close as possible to one-on-one training.

The brand: Fonseca Fitness, built around Pete and Lisa together.
What works now: people trust you because current clients send them your way.
The offer: workout programming, nutrition guidance, weekly check-ins, and exercise help.
The opportunity: online training can be accessible beyond the local area while staying personal.

What we learned

Fonseca Fitness Has Trust. Now It Needs Reach.

Right now, new clients usually come from word of mouth. That means people already trust the work when they hear about it from someone they know.

The problem is not credibility. The problem is scale. In-person training is limited by the number of hours in a day. If one client takes up a large block of time, there are only so many people you can help face to face.

The online side could change that. If the process is simple enough, Fonseca Fitness could support more people with workouts, nutrition, reminders, check-ins, and coaching from home without giving up the one-on-one training style that already works.

"We are not a cookie-cutter training program." That should be the heart of the website and the online coaching offer.

Pain points

What This Should Help With First

The first version should solve real problems without creating another thing to babysit.

Growth

In-person time has a ceiling

You can only train so many people in person during the day. Online coaching could create more room to grow.

Diets

Diet plans are the main thing clients need and the main thing that becomes annoying to repeat.

Reminders

Clients need nudges

Simple reminders could keep clients on track without turning every follow-up into a separate text thread.

Exercises

Some movements need explaining

If a client does not know what an exercise means, the plan should include a quick demo link and simple coaching notes.

Online

The online side is the unknown

The opportunity is clear, but the system needs to make online clients manageable instead of overwhelming.

Personal

The personal approach must stay

The platform should support customized coaching, not make the business feel generic or automated.

The evolved idea

A Website Plus a Simple Online Coaching System

This is no longer just a website and a place to store notes. The stronger idea is a small online training system that starts with the pieces clients already need most.

  • A public website that explains who you are, what you offer, and why your experience matters.
  • A clear online training offer for people who cannot train with you in person.
  • A client login where each person can see their current diet plan and reminders.
  • Workout plans with exercise demo links, so clients are not guessing what a movement means.
  • A simple way for clients to communicate when they need help.
  • A trainer dashboard that shows who needs a diet update, reminder, or check-in.
  • A way to reuse structure without making anyone feel like they got a generic plan.
  • A workflow that can eventually support 20 to 30 online clients from home.

What we know now

The Online Offer Is Taking Shape

This is enough to start thinking about a real offer, but not so much that it becomes complicated.

  • Core offer: workout program, nutrition guidance, and a weekly check-in.
  • Exercise guidance: demo video links plus Pete/Lisa notes for form, cues, and modifications.
  • Check-in details: weight, injuries from training, and an optional progress photo.
  • Communication: one message per day, answered within the agreed response window.
  • Scale rule: the system has to stay simple enough to manage 20 to 30 online clients.
  • Brand direction: Fonseca Fitness, with Pete and Lisa together as the coaching team.
  • Coach fit: the program recommends the right fit instead of making clients choose up front.

There is no reason to build a big system right away. The next steps should prove the business idea before investing in a full portal.

Later

Payments

Payments Can Stay the Way They Are

If Zelle, Venmo, Cash App, checks, or cash are working for you, there is no need to change that at the beginning.

The first question is whether online training can be offered and managed well. Payments can be added later with simple links or tracking only if the convenience is worth the fees.
